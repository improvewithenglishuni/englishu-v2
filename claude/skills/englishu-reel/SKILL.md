# EnglishU Reel

EnglishU is a vocabulary platform for Israeli students preparing for the Psychometric and Amirnet exams. The Reels are its top-of-funnel content: short, genuinely interesting explainers for Hebrew-speaking 18-25 year olds that do NOT feel like English lessons. The viewer learns something surprising first and absorbs one English word almost by accident.

The owner (Sharon) is a teacher, not a video editor, and dictates by voice. Explain in plain language, show results instead of describing them, and let her react to a real mockup before anything costs credits.

## Hard rules

1. **Never spend Higgsfield credits without her explicit approval of a specific plan.** Run free cost checks first (`get_cost: true` on generate_video / generate_image), show a table with totals and the remaining balance, then wait. Approval prompts must also be tapped "Allow" in the app; if a call returns "No approval received", nothing was spent, so tell her and ask her to tap Allow. Free work (editing, audio, text, mockups, end cards) never needs asking.
2. **Never generate text inside AI images or clips.** Hebrew and English come out garbled. All words are added in the edit so the spelling and right-to-left layout are exact.
3. **Do not use a red marker circle as the highlight.** She finds it generic, and a marker baked into a clip cannot be removed. Use the spotlight vignette or the magnifying glass (see `references/visual-style.md`), at most twice per Reel.
4. **Never cut the voiceover inside a word.** Cut only at energy minima in the gaps. Never use heavy noise reduction (it made her voice warble).
5. **No whoosh on every cut.** At most two soft whooshes per Reel. Sound effects are sparse and tied to a label or a key word.
6. **Every shot is a full-bleed collage in one cohesive "world"**, never a rectangular photo sitting on a blank background. This was the main reason Reel 1 (contrails) looked better and performed better than Reel 2 (jeans). Details in `references/visual-style.md`.
7. **The ending must do four jobs** (Sharon's rule for every script). It must be (a) **relatable to an Israeli audience**: a line about the viewer's own life, never "next time you see [something Israelis will not meet]" (she rejected "next time you see an otter"); (b) **intriguing**: it leaves a small spark, a surprising twist or a thought worth sitting with, not a flat wrap-up; (c) **close the educational loop**: it answers the question the hook opened and lands a target word, so the viewer feels the story is complete; and (d) **if possible, give a reason to share**, for example a line they can send to someone ("send this to someone you wouldn't want to drift away from"). The share incentive is a bonus: never force it, and drop it when it would feel like a tacked-on call to action. Never end on a vocabulary definition or an abrupt fact. The picture returns to the hook image. Before approving any script, check its last line against these four jobs.
8. **The body palette comes from the topic's world, never from the brand.** Pick 3-4 colors for the world (a light base, a dark base, cream/white, and ONE accent), the way the plane Reel used sky blue, deep navy, white and one orange. The brand navy and red appear only on the logo and the end card. A Reel whose body is brand navy plus red is wrong: Sharon rejected it as "not Vox". This rule overrides the palette table and the "do not use yellow" line in `references/visual-style.md`; that line pushed builds toward a generic brand look, and a world may use any accent that suits it.
9. **One locked world per Reel.** All scenes share one background plate (plus at most one dark variant for the science/mechanism beats). Scenes change only the layers on top. Never switch between flat navy, cream, dot grids and sepia photos from scene to scene.
10. **No flat fields and no bare rectangles.** Every frame is full-bleed layered paper with visible texture. A flat colour block, an empty navy screen or a clean rectangular photo on a plain background counts as a failed frame.
11. **Length gate before building.** The narration is at most about 90 Hebrew words (about 35 seconds at her pace) and at most 8 beats, built around ONE surprising fact. If the research gives more (history, mechanism, a timeline and a human story), pick the single most surprising thread and drop the rest, or split it into two Reels. The smallpox cut ran 75 seconds because it tried to tell three stories.
12. **Reference-first.** Before designing or building any new Reel, extract 10-12 frames from an approved Reel (the plane Reel, Higgsfield media id `4651ec12-9351-404d-a086-0b8a3ecb1347`, 34.5 s, 1080 x 1920; a second 34.5 s upload `2e93fd88-e594-41c6-b2a5-4e55e3f5e924` looks like a version of it) and look at them. Claude has no taste of its own for this style; it copies what it has just seen.

## Standard production workflow (Nov 2026; overrides the older Higgsfield workflow below)

Three stages. Sharon approves each one before the next starts. No stage spends Higgsfield credits.

1. **Script.** Write one comprehensive script for the whole Reel. Limits: under 40 s in total (story about 33 s plus the 2.7 s end card), about 90 words at most (Hebrew plus English), at most 9 beats, ONE surprising fact, and exactly TWO target words (one B2 and one C1, mostly nouns and verbs, pitched high: she rejected NAVIGATE and COMMUNICATE as too easy). Web-search every fact. The script must carry all the attention-getters: an open-loop hook in the first 2-3 s that shows the real image whole and then zooms ~300% onto the detail (zoom plus a yellow glow ring, not a red marker circle); a mini-question or second reveal in the middle, ideally a twist that corrects the popular version of the fact; a new visual every 1.5-4 s; push and pull between full-frame real photos and animated cutout graphics; the English sentence for the first target word about one third in, the second word later; both target words acted out on screen with the Hebrew gloss on screen only; a reflective callback that loops to the hook; and a red block-letter stamp restating a target word at the end. Deliver (a) the recording script, which is also the exact subtitle text, (b) a scene table (second, on screen, image type), (c) accuracy notes with sources, and (d) open decisions. Save it to the project as `claude/<topic>-reel-script.md`. Check word levels in `/mnt/project/Master_Word_Bank_Difficulty.csv`; if the file is missing, mark the levels unverified and ask once for it.
2. **Mock-up (Claude).** Build a silent, simplified Remotion mock-up of the whole Reel, drawn in code, so she can react to the story, pacing and the two word animations. Follow the script beat for beat, use one typeface and the Vox plates, and say plainly what is missing (real photos, sound, end card). Render a contact sheet of stills before the full render. She gives notes one beat at a time: apply each change to the latest approved version and save it as a new version (v1, v2, v3...), changing nothing else, and keep the old versions.
3. **Recreate and refine in Claude Code.** Once the mock-up is approved, rebuild the Reel in Claude Code as a proper Remotion project with sharp real images, her own voiceover (retime to it), sound and the end card. She then edits manually by talking to Claude Code until she is happy. Run the per-frame QA gate (see "Building in Remotion / Claude Code") before each delivery.

### Edit-to-guideline loop
After every round of Sharon's notes, and again when a Reel is finished, list every edit she asked for and sort it. A **one-off** (one colour in one scene, a taste call for this topic) stays in that Reel. A **general** edit (it would have been right for any Reel) becomes one line, written as rule plus reason, in the matching section of this skill, added in the same turn with the skill-update card. If a new guideline contradicts an old rule, replace the old rule and say so instead of stacking both. Never promote a guess about her taste; promote only what she asked for or reacted to.

### Guidelines promoted from edits so far
- **Accuracy is absolute.** Every word, label, number, date, caption and image must be true and sourced. Popular-but-overstated facts get hedged in the script ("sometimes", "in the wild it's rare") and never shown in a way that implies the false version. Never show a real photo that implies something untrue (for example a lone pup suggesting abandonment).
- **Colour a target word as one unit.** Never colour only part of a word (she asked why only "VEY" in CONVEY was orange; it read as a mistake). Split colours only when the narration names that part and it is labelled. The accent belongs to the effect (the signal, the stamp, the ring), not to arbitrary letters.
- **Act each target word out as a small cause-and-effect story** with the topic's own characters, not a decorative text effect. Example: the dancing bee sends a signal, the letters of CONVEY ride it one by one in reading order, the bees that receive it react, then the word assembles and the Hebrew gloss lands. Opposite words get opposite motion (DRIFT pulls apart, BOND links).
- **Moving letters must stay readable.** Large enough to read while they travel, slow enough that each letter gets time, and the finished word held at least 3 s.
- **Abstract stand-ins must look like the idea.** If a word means encoded, show encoded things (hex, binary and symbol tiles that decode into the letters), not generic squiggles.
- **In a Hebrew Reel, motion and sequence flow right to left.** Put the source on the right and the destination on the left, and start timelines and lists from the right.
- **Zoom only where it is needed.** No automatic push or zoom on every scene (Remotion `Scene` default `push` must be set to `[1, 1]`). Exception: a full-bleed real photo that fills the whole frame gets a slow zoom (about 6 percent over the scene), because a static full-frame photo feels dead (Sharon asked for this). Stickers on plates, portraits, word slides and charts stay still; a chart's own animation is enough. Other zooms only where they carry the meaning: the hook close-up on the detail, a ring-vaccination style scene, a map zoom to one place. Sharon noticed and rejected a subtle zoom on every card. A target word and its gloss stay fixed in place once their entrance animation ends. This replaces the older "slow push on every scene" notes below.
- **Close the loop with a callback animation.** When the ending returns to the hook object, animate it (for example the arm with the vaccine scar raising into a strong-arm "Rosie the Riveter" pose to say we won), not just a repeated still.
- **One change per round.** Edit only the beat she named, on the newest approved version, and keep every earlier version.
- **Name the topic in the first two or three lines, and use one name for it all the way through.** A hook that says "such a disease" leaves the viewer not knowing what the story is about. When the English term is the name she wants (smallpox), say it in English and never alternate with the Hebrew name; switching between the two names confused her.
- **Open with a question or a puzzle, not a fact.** Line 1 asks something the viewer wants answered ("Did you notice grandma has a scar on her arm?") and line 2 answers it. A purely factual first line felt flat; she wanted intrigue.
- **Chain the lines so each one hands off to the next.** Use a connector (כי, ומכאן, אבל, והיום?) or a question the next line answers, and let one sentence run across two takes (record those as flowing clauses). Lines that read as separate facts felt "detached" to her.
- **Every line must be understood on a single listen.** No same verb twice in a row, no clipped names ("בשחורות" for the full disease name), and both sides of a contrast stated outright. She could not follow a line that broke these.
- **Say each fact once.** When a Hebrew line and the English target sentence carry the same fact (a year), delete the Hebrew line and put the fact in the English sentence, which is the vocabulary payoff. The repeat only slowed the beat.
- **A refinement keeps the same beats and the same running time.** Trade words inside the lines; do not add lines or stretch the Reel (her brief when she asked for a more refined script).
- **Her recorded wording is the master script.** When she sends a recording, transcribe it, use the last complete take of each line, and make that transcript the script. Change a line only when she asks or when it is an accuracy or clarity problem, and raise those as flagged questions, not silent edits. End with the list of lines she must re-record.
- **Show the whole current script in the chat reply every time it changes** (numbered, with the changed lines named after it), and also save it to the project as a new version. She asked for "the updated script here" when she got only notes and a file.

### Current look and build notes (Nov 2026; where these conflict with Hard rules 3, 8, 10 and 12 or `references/visual-style.md`, this section wins)
- Vox plates, no flat fields: light `#EDEEF3`, dark `#3A3C4A`, periwinkle `#A1ACCA`, charcoal `#353336`, paper `#F4F3F9`, ONE warm accent coral `#E0615E`, highlight yellow `#FDF551` (glows and rings only), stamp red `#E5372F`. Brand navy and red only on the logo and end card. Hard cuts between scenes, each with its own plate; no automatic push (see "Zoom only where it is needed"). The palette may switch to the Vox set (ink black, field-guide grey, white line, Vox yellow `#F7E21B` as the one accent) when the inspiration calls for it; see `references/vox-aesthetics.md`.
- **Reuse a background only if it is visually general.** A plate can carry over to a new Reel only when nothing about it belongs to the world built for an earlier Reel: smooth ink-black dry brush, mottled field-guide grey, plain newsprint are general; the crumpled/creased paper with 3D folds from the bee Reel is that Reel's world and must not be reused (Sharon asked to drop it from the otter Reel). Reason: a distinctive plate makes every new Reel look like a sequel of the old one and breaks Hard rule 9 (one world per topic). When in doubt, build a new plate for the topic.
- **Balance graphics with real-world images and footage.** The Vox look is the collaboration of the two, never one alone: real photos and real video carry proof and emotion, while hand-drawn lines, labels, cutouts and animated diagrams carry the explanation on top of them. Aim for roughly half the beats led by real media (full-frame photo, footage, archival document or engraving) and half led by graphics (scribble animation, annotation, kinetic word), and let them touch inside the same frame: draw the yellow ring, kelp line or arrows ON the real photo, put the real photo inside the graphic as a torn clipping. A Reel that is all drawn code (the mock-up) or all stock photos fails this rule. Sources: free public-domain or CC photos and video (Wikimedia Commons, USFWS/NOAA b-roll, old naturalist engravings), fetched through the Higgsfield sandbox courier, never credits without approval. Keep a credits list.
- **Typography: two families, Vox pairing** (replaces the older "one typeface per video" rule). A heavy grotesque sans for headlines, target words and labels (Rubik 900 covers Hebrew and Latin), and a high-contrast Didone serif, mostly italic, for captions, sources, glosses and dates (Playfair Display Italic 700/900 for English, Frank Ruhl Libre 700/900 for Hebrew, never a fake italic on Hebrew). Subtitles stay Rubik. Full font notes in `references/vox-aesthetics.md`.
- Images are crisp or small, never blurry; full-frame or die-cut stickers, never a wide band with empty margins; every image must visualize its line; include a chart when there is a key quantity. Keep content above y = 1480 (only subtitles below) and the right 130 px clear.
- Full details live in the project doc `claude/vox-reel-blueprint.md`; read it before building. The inspiration aesthetics (magazine cutout, painted field guide, ink line art, scribble animation, document evidence, annotated footage) are catalogued in `references/vox-aesthetics.md`; read it before designing scenes.
- Remotion in the Claude sandbox: Chrome at `/usr/bin/google-chrome`, `Config.setChromiumOpenGlRenderer('swangle')`, 2 CPU cores so render with `--concurrency=2` in the background and poll the log, fonts from `@fontsource/rubik` (Hebrew and Latin subsets with unicode-range), stills at `--scale=0.5` for checks. The Claude container cannot download photos (Wikimedia rate-limits it, most hosts are blocked), so use the Higgsfield sandbox courier: `sandbox_exec` has internet, search Commons with the API (sleep 3-4 s between calls), download thumbs at width 1600-1920, trim video with ffmpeg there, tar everything, `media_upload` a `.tar` general file, PUT it from the sandbox, `media_confirm` type `file`, then `curl` the cloudfront URL from the Claude container. No credits are spent.
- Transcribing her recordings in the Claude sandbox: HuggingFace is blocked, but `pip install sherpa-onnx` plus the `sherpa-onnx-whisper-medium` archive from the k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx GitHub release tag `asr-models` works. Convert to 16 kHz mono wav, split on silence, decode with `language='he'` and `set_option("max_tokens_per_second","25")` (the default cuts lines off), and re-decode English lines with `language='en'`. Treat the result as a draft: flag doubtful words and ask her to confirm them.

## The look to reproduce (read from the plane Reel's frames)

This is the look Sharon loves; every build, in any tool, is judged against it.

- **World.** Sky beats: crumpled sky-blue paper with visible 3D creases and patches of halftone dots; clouds as paper cutouts with white edges and soft shadows; the contrail is literally a torn white paper strip. Mechanism beats: deep navy paper with a lighter torn-paper layer, ice crystals as small paper hexagons, orange as a paper shape, thin arrows. Everything runs to the edges of the frame.
- **Palette.** Four colors and one accent: sky blue, deep navy, white/cream, plus a single orange. The accent appears only on the key word (the TRAIL half of CON-TRAIL), one hand-drawn swoosh or underline, arrows and one thin ring. No brand red or brand navy anywhere in the body.
- **Cutouts.** Subjects (planes, engine, crystals) are real-looking black-and-white or muted photo cutouts with a thin white paper border and a soft drop shadow, slightly rotated, never clean rectangles.
- **Text.** A torn cream paper label rotated a few degrees with dark text for the hook; the key English word in big condensed Anton, white with a dark outline, the target half in the accent; the word splits or scatters to act out its meaning (DISPERSE). Hebrew subtitles are small, white, bottom-aligned inside the safe area.
- **Motion.** The world stays put while elements slide, pop and scatter on top of it, with a slow camera push; crystals fly apart on arrows, the plane slides across the plate. It reads as one continuous shot, not a slideshow of separate pictures.

## Building in Remotion / Claude Code (what changed and why)

Remotion is an animation and layout engine, not an art department. Left alone it draws flat colour blocks and clean rectangles in whatever palette it was given, which is exactly what went wrong in the smallpox build. The plane look was generated pixels (Higgsfield with the style reference). So:

1. **Art from Higgsfield or ChatGPT, motion from code.** Generate the world plates (one light, one dark) and the cutout sheets with the master prompt in `references/visual-style.md` (plates plus cutouts), and remove backgrounds with the Higgsfield `remove_background` tool or an equivalent. Remotion only composes and animates them, adds subtitles and renders. Public-domain photos are fine as cutout sources, but they must pass through the cutout recipe below.
2. **Three layers per scene, one shared plate.** Layer 1 is the locked plate (it never changes between scenes and takes the slow camera push). Layer 2 is mid-ground paper shapes and halftone, black-and-white cutouts. Layer 3 is the foreground key object, labels and the key word. Keep one folder of shared assets and one component file per scene.
3. **Cutout recipe (every image).** Background removed, converted to high-contrast black and white or muted colour, halftone applied in a pre-processing step (Python or the sandbox), an 8-12 px white paper border with an irregular torn edge (SVG clip-path), a soft shadow, a rotation of -4 to +4 degrees.
4. **Texture stack over everything.** A paper-grain overlay at 6-10 percent (an SVG turbulence filter or a grain PNG), a very light vignette, and halftone dots on at least one layer per scene. If a still frame looks like a clean vector template, the texture is missing.
5. **Motion language.** Use spring for pop-ins and interpolate for pushes and pans. Stagger entrances by 4-6 frames, rotate pop-ins slightly, do not push the plate on every scene (slow zoom only on full-frame images, other zooms only where they carry meaning), and give hand-drawn accents (swoosh, underline, arrows) a draw-on animation. Expose scale, x and y as Remotion Studio props for every element so Sharon or Claude can nudge and save positions without rewriting code.
6. **Sound.** Voice is always hers. Keep the existing sparse SFX rule (no whoosh per cut, at most two soft whooshes), but tie a soft paper or pop sound to key element entrances; sound is about half of perceived quality.
7. **Per-frame QA gate before delivery.** Render a contact sheet and answer yes or no: (a) is every frame full-bleed layered paper with visible texture, (b) is there one world and one palette of 3-4 colors plus one accent, (c) is the brand navy/red absent from the body, (d) are all photos cutouts with a white border and shadow, (e) does something new appear every 1.5-4 seconds, (f) is it 35-40 seconds including the end card. Fix every "no" before sending.

## Reel anatomy (summary)

- Hook in the first 2 seconds that challenges an assumption.
- Reveal, then mechanism, then a second reveal or question, then the payoff.
- One complete English sentence containing the target word, about one third of the way in. The Hebrew translation appears on screen only, never spoken.
- Hebrew narration almost continuous, gaps of about 0.3 s, so something new is said or shown every 1.5-4 seconds.
- Reflective or shareable ending that loops back to the opening image. The audience is in Israel, so never end on "next time you see [a thing they will never meet]" (she rejected "next time you see an otter"). End on a line about the viewer's own life, ideally one they can act on ("send this to someone you wouldn't want to drift away from"), tied to a target word, while the picture returns to the hook image.
- Animated end card (about 2.7 s) with her logo and handle.
- Vertical 1080 x 1920, 30 fps, about 30-37 s of story plus the end card. No AI voice, no stock music (original synthesized bed only).
