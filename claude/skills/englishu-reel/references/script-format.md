## Structure and timing

Aim for roughly 30-34 seconds of narration at natural pace (the edit speeds her voice up about 5%). Hebrew reads slower than it looks on paper, so a 30-second script often records at 34-40 seconds. Flag pacing problems before production and offer shorter wording rather than asking her to rush.

| Beat | Purpose | Typical time |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | A surprising claim or a question about something the viewer can see right now ("look at your jeans, see that tiny pocket?"). Say the surprise, not the answer. | 0-4 s |
| First reveal | The surprising fact | 4-8 s |
| Mechanism | How or why, in one or two sentences | 8-16 s |
| English sentence | Target word inside a full natural sentence | about one third in |
| Second reveal or question | "So why did it stay?" Gives a reason to keep watching | middle |
| Payoff | Answers the second question | 20-28 s |
| Reflective ending | Loops back to the hook ("next time you look up / put on jeans...") | last 4-6 s |

What worked: the plane Reel opened with a claim ("this plane will land in an hour and its trail will still be in the sky") and delayed the explanation; the final reveal (some clouds were made by planes) gave a shareable last line. What didn't: starting with the answer ("it's not smoke") gives away the payoff in the first second.

## The English sentence

- One complete sentence, natural and slightly slower than the Hebrew, with the target word stressed: "Jeans have retained this tiny pocket ever since."
- Place it about a third of the way in so the vocabulary is introduced after the viewer is hooked but well before the ending. Placing it last felt like a lesson and was too late.
- Never define the word out loud. Show the Hebrew meaning as an on-screen label, for example `retain = לשמר / להשאיר`, in dictionary form so it cannot be read as a command.
- The word must feel earned by the story. Choose a word whose meaning the visuals can demonstrate (DISPERSE = crystals drift apart; RETAIN = the pocket stays while everything around it changes).
- When a second word is useful (TRAIL in the plane Reel), introduce it as a quick discovery inside a naming moment ("contrail, short for condensation trail"), not as a definition.

## Endings

- Reflective and warm, slightly slower, ending on a falling tone. Examples: "next time you look up, think: maybe that cloud started as a plane's white trail"; "next time you put on jeans, look at that tiny pocket... and remember you're wearing a small piece of history."
- Avoid: ending on a fact with no landing, repeating an earlier line almost word for word, ending on the English definition.
- If a closing line feels abrupt in the recording, lengthen it so it flows out of the pause (add a connecting word such as "ותזכרו") rather than adding more content.

## Fact-checking and word choice

- Run a quick web search before locking the script. Fix numbers ("almost 150 years" was wrong for 1873; the script says "more than 150 years"). Prefer hedged wording when a cause is partial ("among other things").
- Never invent facts. Avoid real famous people and brand logos in visuals.
- Target word: check it in `/mnt/project/Master_Word_Bank_Difficulty.csv`. Words already used: TRAIL, DISPERSE, RETAIN.
- Audience is mid-20s students in Israel: choose topics with local relevance or broad curiosity (geography, history, tech, culture, conspiracy that resolves into a real fact).

## Review checklist

- [ ] Hook challenges an assumption in the first 2 seconds
- [ ] Narration nearly continuous, no dead stretches
- [ ] English sentence about one third in, translation on screen only
- [ ] A second question or reveal in the middle
- [ ] Payoff answers it explicitly
- [ ] Reflective ending that loops to the hook
- [ ] Every number checked; hedges where needed
- [ ] Visual direction written for each line (see `visual-style.md`)

## Delivery tips to give her before recording

Give the final script in one block she can read from, plus line-by-line tips:

- Talk to one curious friend, smile slightly on the hook, stress one word per sentence.
- Keep gaps between sentences short (about half a second); record 2-3 full takes and send the most natural.
- If she gets distracted she can restart the sentence; the edit uses only complete takes. If she re-records one line separately, she should say which file has the newer version.
- Quiet, soft room, phone a hand-width from the mouth, slightly to the side. Standing helps.
- English line: slow down slightly on the target word, let it breathe.
- Ending line: relaxed and warm, falling tone, a real pause at any "...".
